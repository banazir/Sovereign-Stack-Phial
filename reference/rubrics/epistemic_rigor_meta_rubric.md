# 🧭 META-RUBRIC: Epistemic Rigor & Structural Containment

**Document Purpose:** 
A topic-agnostic evaluation matrix designed to score candidate instruction sets, degree programs, or academic syllabi. This meta-rubric evaluates whether a curriculum successfully transitions students from passive, stochastic consumption of information into rigorous, deterministic, and structurally contained scientific reasoning.

## 📊 Evaluation Criteria & Scoring Weights (100-Point Scale)

| Criterion | Weight | Focus Area |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[1] A Priori Hypothesis / Constraint Verification** | 30 pts | Rejection of post-hoc rationalization; enforcement of pre-defined bounds before execution. |
| **[2] Scalable Epistemic Architecture** | 25 pts | Progression from isolated "toy" problems to interconnected, bounded systems. |
| **[3] Epistemic Distinctions (Generator vs. Verifier)** | 20 pts | Explicit separation of stochastic ideation from deterministic, mathematical/empirical validation. |
| **[4] Structural Containment of Divergence** | 15 pts | Mapping theoretical frameworks to actionable, physically blocking verification artifacts. |
| **[5] Failure Mode & Adversarial Analysis Bias** | 10 pts | Active engineering defenses against methodological collapse, drift, or systemic defects. |

---

### 1. A Priori Hypothesis / Constraint Verification (30 Points)
*Does the curriculum force students to define the boundary conditions of success and failure before executing the work?*

* **1 – Fatal Misalignment (0–6 pts):** Accepts post-hoc documentation, narrative summaries of existing work, or unstructured "exploration" as valid academic artifacts. Fails to require verification against a baseline.
* **3 – Partial Differentiation (12–18 pts):** Requires hypotheses or design documents, but accepts informal "acceptance criteria" without requiring mathematical, logical, or rigorous empirical proofs.
* **5 – Sovereign Consonance (24–30 pts):** Strictly enforces that constraints (bounds, null hypotheses, boundary conditions) must be defined *before* execution. Requires the curriculum to include layers that algorithmically or empirically prove the output meets the pre-defined constraints.

### 2. Scalable Epistemic Architecture (25 Points)
*Does the curriculum graduate beyond single-node tasks into the orchestration and evaluation of complex systems?*

* **1 – Fatal Misalignment (0–5 pts):** Evaluates students only on isolated, standalone scripts, essays, or tasks with no systemic integration.
* **3 – Partial Differentiation (10–15 pts):** Acknowledges multi-part systems or broad theories but treats them as monolithic tasks without enforcing rigorous interfaces between the parts.
* **5 – Sovereign Consonance (20–25 pts):** Explicitly teaches the specification of contracts, interfaces, or interdisciplinary boundaries. The curriculum must govern how different components, datasets, or methodologies interact and validate one another.

### 3. Epistemic Distinctions (Generator vs. Verifier) (20 Points)
*Does the curriculum teach the fundamental physical difference between stochastic ideation (or LLM generation) and deterministic validation?*

* **1 – Fatal Misalignment (0–4 pts):** Treats generative tools or human intuition as omnipotent. Fails to address hallucination, cognitive bias, or the Open World Assumption.
* **3 – Partial Differentiation (8–12 pts):** Acknowledges flaws in generation/ideation but relies on unstructured human visual inspection or simple peer review as the only verification mechanism.
* **5 – Sovereign Consonance (16–20 pts):** Maps the rigid epistemic boundary: Ideation and Generation are Stochastic; Evaluation and Baselining are Deterministic. Requires a separate, rigorous engine (formal methods, empirical testing, double-blind review) to evaluate the generated output.

### 4. Structural Containment of Divergence (15 Points)
*Does the curriculum translate theoretical frameworks into concrete, verifiable, and physically blocking artifacts?*

* **1 – Fatal Misalignment (0–3 pts):** Accepts theoretical essays or comparative reviews as final deliverables without requiring working implementations or reproducible data.
* **5 – Sovereign Consonance (12–15 pts):** Specifications/Hypotheses must be tied to actionable GitOps artifacts, Pull Requests, continuous integration (CI) tests, or pre-registered trial structures that physically block completion if the specification is violated.

### 5. Failure Mode & Adversarial Analysis Bias (10 Points)
*Does the curriculum ensure students critically engage with systemic failure, algorithmic drift, and the limitations of their own methodologies?*

* **1 – Fatal Misalignment (0–2 pts):** Operates on toxic optimism, ignoring the compounding risks of unverified outputs, biased data, or systemic failure.
* **5 – Sovereign Consonance (8–10 pts):** Requires the syllabus to actively study and specify constraints around failure modes, state collapse, context exhaustion, and adversarial or null results within the domain.
